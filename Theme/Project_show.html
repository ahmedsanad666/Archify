Obsidian House

Interior Design
Client: Private Commission
Location: Kyoto, Japan
Year: 2024

A brutalist sanctuary carved from shadows and silence, nestled in the ancient hills of Kyoto.

The Obsidian House explores the dialogue between stark permanence and transient natural light. Designed as a retreat for an art collector, the spatial sequence is orchestrated to frame select vistas of the surrounding bamboo forest while maintaining an introspective, almost monastic atmosphere within.

Materiality drives the narrative. Board-formed concrete, blackened steel, and charred cedar (Shou Sugi Ban) absorb light, allowing the carefully curated illumination—both natural and artificial—to sculpt the volumes. The absence of color forces focus onto texture, temperature, and the subtle shifts of daylight across the heavy surfaces.

The layout abandons traditional domestic boundaries, favoring fluid, interconnected zones separated only by structural massive piers and subtle level changes, encouraging a deliberate, slow pace of living.
